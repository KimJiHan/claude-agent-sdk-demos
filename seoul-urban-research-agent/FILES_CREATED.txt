KEY FINDINGS SUMMARY:
====================

1. INFRASTRUCTURE FIRST
   - Amsterdam (50% modal share): 50+ years, 500+ km network
   - Copenhagen (45% modal share): 30+ years, segregated infrastructure
   - Berlin (18%→23%): 12 years, 3,000 km target
   - Tokyo (10%, stagnant): Enforcement without infrastructure fails

2. CLIMATE ADAPTATION POSSIBLE
   - Winter (-10°C): Proven in Copenhagen/Berlin (95%+ annual operation)
   - Monsoon: Requires drainage over-engineering (+15-20% cost)
   - Summer (+33°C): Manageable with shade and cooling surfaces
   - Cost: +10-20% for climate adaptation (necessary, not optional)

3. KOREA'S REALISTIC TARGETS (15 years)
   - Modal share: 15-20% (not 45% like Copenhagen)
   - Investment: €400-600 million (€27-40M annually)
   - Network: 800+ km comprehensive system
   - Safety: 70% accident reduction through design
   - Environmental: 2-3M tonnes CO₂ avoided annually

4. AVOID TOKYO'S FAILURE
   - 67,000 bicycle accidents/year despite ¥1M penalties
   - Root cause: Minimal infrastructure (Tokyo has 1/10 of NYC's bike lanes)
   - Solution: Infrastructure first, then regulations (Korea must follow Europe model)

5. INTEGRATION IS KEY
   - Ttareungyi (3,000+ stations) as cycling hubs
   - Transit connection (first/last mile solution)
   - Land-use planning (Han River, mixed-use development)
   - Tech integration (app, real-time systems)

CLIMATE ADAPTATIONS REQUIRED:
=============================

Winter (-10°C):
- High-friction asphalt, enhanced drainage, 3m+ width
- Priority snow removal (before main roads)
- User support: E-bike subsidies, weather shelters
- Modal impact: 20-30% reduction in harsh months

Monsoon (Critical):
- Drainage over-engineering (porous asphalt, 1% grade minimum)
- Elevated routes in flood zones
- Real-time flood monitoring, emergency procedures
- Cost: +15-20% infrastructure premium
- Modal impact: 15-25% reduction during heavy rain

Summer (+33°C):
- Shade integration, cooler asphalt, water stations
- Modest cost, manageable with design
- User support: Heat alerts
