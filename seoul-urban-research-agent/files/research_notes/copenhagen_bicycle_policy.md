# Copenhagen: Bicycle Infrastructure Policy Analysis

## Overview
Copenhagen is recognized as the world's leading cycling city, with comprehensive infrastructure, safety standards, and the highest cycling modal share among major global cities.

## Road Expansion Strategy

### Strategic Goals and Timeline
- **Goal**: 50% modal share for commuter trips (2025 target)
- **Safety Target**: 70% reduction in serious cycling injuries
- **Vision**: Best cycling city in the world

### Network Components

#### Cycle Track Prioritization Plan (2017-2025)
- Creates coherent and attractive cycling network
- Prioritizes connections with greatest accessibility value
- Focus on safety and perceived safety improvements
- Network expansion to improve regional connectivity

#### Infrastructure Types
1. **Kerb-Segregated Cycle Tracks**: ~382 km
2. **On-Street Cycle Lanes**: ~33 km
3. **Off-Street Green Routes**: ~63 km through parks and green areas
4. **Bicycle Superhighways**: Regional network connecting cities with few stops

#### PLUS-Net (Prioritized Network)
- High requirements for track width, maintenance, snow clearance
- Focus on busiest corridors
- Enhanced safety features at intersections

## Safety Standards

### Design Standards (Updated 2024)
- **Minimum Width**: 1.7 meters legally, but 2.2-2.5 meters standard practice
- **High-Traffic Areas**: 3-4 meters wide
- Modern design manual updated to handle:
  - Increased cyclist numbers
  - Cargo bikes and e-bikes
  - Improved intersection designs
  - Safer school routes with speed reduction measures

### Safety Infrastructure
- Redesigned high-accident intersections
- Green waves synchronized to cycling speeds
- Bicycle and pedestrian-only bridges over busy streets and waterways
- Speed reduction measures on residential streets
- Improved crossing designs at all intersections

### Safety Performance
- **72% reduction in cycling accidents** since 1996
- Serious injuries: decreased from 252 (1996) to 78 (2018)
- Fatal incidents: dramatically reduced (3 deaths in 2010)
- **Safety metric**: Average cyclist travels 4.4 million km before serious accident

## Resident Usage and Modal Share

### Modal Share Statistics
- **45% of all trips** to work and study by bicycle
- Growth trajectory: 31% (1995) → 49% (2018) → targeting 50%
- Primary transport for most age groups including children, workers, elderly

### Usage Patterns
- High consistency across seasons (winter cycling infrastructure supports year-round use)
- Strong commuter cycling culture
- Integration with public transport (bike-train combinations)

### Infrastructure Supporting High Usage
- Extensive secure parking facilities
- Weather-protected waiting areas
- Service points and repair facilities
- Intersection designs optimized for high cyclist volumes

## Traffic Congestion and Environmental Impact

### Congestion Reduction
- High cycling modal share (45%+) significantly reduces car traffic
- Cycling provides capacity equivalent to new road construction
- Reduced need for parking infrastructure compared to car-dependent cities
- Smooth traffic flow through efficient intersection management

### Environmental Benefits
- Eliminates car emissions for ~45% of trips
- Significant reduction in air pollution and particulate matter
- Noise reduction in urban areas
- Reduced urban heat island effect
- Lower overall carbon footprint for transport sector

## Policy and Investment

### Investment and Funding
- Major structural investments in cycle infrastructure since 1980s
- Annual maintenance budget for winter snow clearance of cycle tracks
- Dedicated funding for intersection redesigns
- Investment in bicycle parking at transit hubs

### Policy Framework
- Long-term commitment with consistent political support
- Integration with urban planning, health, and climate strategies
- Focus on social equity and accessibility
- Coordination with regional partners for bicycle superhighways

### Implementation Approach
- Incremental expansion with high standards
- Community engagement and feedback
- Evidence-based improvements (accident data analysis)
- Continuous refinement of design standards
