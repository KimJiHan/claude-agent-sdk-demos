# Other Bicycle-Friendly Cities: Policy Analysis

## New York City

### Policy Framework
- **NYC Streets Plan**: 2022-2026 implementation, $904 million investment
- **Target**: 250 miles of protected bike lanes
- **Annual Requirement**: Minimum 50 miles per year (2023-2026)

### Performance and Results (2024)
- **2024 Construction**: 29 miles of protected bike lanes (below 50-mile target)
- **Cumulative Progress**: 87.5 miles over 3 years + 20+ miles upgraded
- **Modal Share**: Growing from low base, but gaining momentum
- **Public Support**: 66% support for bike lanes, 72% support for bikeshare

### Infrastructure Features
- **Width Variation**: 6-10 feet depending on location
- **Recent Projects**: First, Second, Sixth, Seventh, and Tenth Avenues improvements
- **Bikeshare**: CitiBike system with high public approval
- **Timeline Challenge**: Behind schedule on annual construction targets

### Challenges
- Implementation slower than policy mandates
- Political resistance and neighborhood concerns
- Tension between traffic flow and cycling safety

## Tokyo

### Infrastructure Status
- **Limited Network**: One-tenth of NYC's and one-twentieth of London's on-street bike facilities
- **Predominant Infrastructure**: Painted lane markings (often blocked by cars)
- **Sidewalk Cycling**: High prevalence due to unsafe road conditions

### Safety Standards and Regulations
- **New Enforcement (2024)**: Stricter regulations with steep penalties
- **Red Ticket Penalties**:
  - Smartphone use: up to ¥100,000 fine
  - Drunk riding: up to ¥1 million fine or 5 years prison
- **Motorist Standards**: Safe distance passing requirements (¥5,000-9,000 fines)
- **Blue Ticket System** (April 2026): Sidewalk cycling fines under dangerous conditions

### Challenges
- **High Accident Rate**: 67,000+ bicycle accidents (2024), 80%+ fatalities involve law violations
- **Infrastructure Gap**: Significantly less safe infrastructure than European cities
- **Cultural Barriers**: High sidewalk cycling due to safety concerns
- **Enforcement Focus**: Penalties for riders rather than comprehensive infrastructure

### Growth Potential
- Despite limited infrastructure, significant casual cycling prevalence
- Recently enhanced safety regulations suggest infrastructure expansion may follow
- Population density could support higher cycling rates with proper infrastructure

## Singapore

### Strategic Plan

#### Islandwide Cycling Network (ICN) Programme
- **Target**: 1,300 km of cycling paths by 2030
- **Current**: 600+ km of cycling paths and connectors
- **Expansion**: Ongoing in multiple neighborhoods (2024+)

#### 2024 Expansion Areas
- Bukit Merah, Telok Blangah, Kallang
- Geylang, Hougang, Kaki Bukit
- Marine Parade, Potong Pasir, Sengkang, Serangoon

### Design and Safety Standards
- **Walking and Cycling Design Guide (WCDG)**: Established 2018 by URA
- **Pedestrian Safety**: 2024 focus on separating cyclists from pedestrians
- **Pedestrian-Only Paths**: Converting footpaths next to bike paths
- **Non-Motorized PMD Rules**: Restrictions on powered devices to enhance safety

### Supporting Infrastructure
- **Parking Expansion**: 3,000 new bike lots at MRT stations (by 2025)
- **Urban Integration**: Cycling integrated with public transit
- **Equity Approach**: Design focuses on sidewalk-based networks for equity

### Approach
- Systematic, planned expansion tied to urban development
- Safety prioritization through design separation
- Integration with public transit system
- Urban planning coordination (URA involvement)

## Key Differences Among Cities

### Infrastructure Maturity
1. **Amsterdam/Copenhagen**: Highly developed, 50+ year commitment
2. **Berlin**: Rapid expansion with ambitious targets
3. **NYC**: Growing but implementation challenges
4. **Tokyo**: Minimal dedicated infrastructure
5. **Singapore**: Systematic growth with transit integration

### Safety Approach
- **European Cities**: Infrastructure-based (segregation, design)
- **Tokyo**: Regulation-based (enforcement, penalties)
- **Singapore**: Design standards with separation from pedestrians
- **NYC**: Mixed approach (infrastructure + regulations developing)

### Modal Share Context
- **Amsterdam**: 50%+ (highest globally)
- **Copenhagen**: 45%+ (second highest)
- **Berlin**: 18%, targeting 23%
- **NYC**: <2% (rapidly growing)
- **Tokyo**: ~10% despite minimal infrastructure
- **Singapore**: <5% but growing rapidly

### Scalability Considerations
- **Space**: Tokyo and Singapore are high-density, suitable for bike lanes
- **Climate**: Wide variation in winter conditions (Berlin, Copenhagen harsh; Asian cities milder)
- **Wealth**: Economic capacity influences investment levels
- **Governance**: Centralized planning (Singapore) vs. distributed (NYC)
